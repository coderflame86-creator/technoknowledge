In January 2026, Pakistan's National Curriculum Council notified a new **AI and robotics curriculum for Grades 1 to 12**. Since then, Islamabad, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Punjab have all announced plans to bring artificial intelligence into their schools.

For principals and school owners, the question is no longer *whether* to teach AI, coding and robotics, but *how to do it well*. This guide sums up what has been announced so far and gives a practical plan for getting your school ready.

## 1. What has been announced?

**The national curriculum (January 2026).** The National Curriculum Council (NCC) of the Ministry of Federal Education and Professional Training notified an AI and robotics curriculum for Grades 1–12. It is built as a step-by-step learning pathway and focuses on:

- computational thinking
- automation
- intelligent systems
- robotics design
- ethical and responsible use of technology
- real-world problem solving

The ministry said the curriculum would be shared with provincial education departments, curriculum bodies and teacher education institutions for orientation, capacity building and phased implementation. ([APP](https://www.app.com.pk/national/ai-robotics-curriculum-notified-for-grades-1-12/), [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/pakistan-introduces-ai-robotics-curriculum-for-for-grades-1-12/))

**Islamabad.** Dawn reported that Islamabad's schools and colleges would start teaching AI from the academic session beginning in April 2026: as a non-credit subject without exams in Grades 1–5, as a choice between computer science and AI in Grades 6–8, and as an elective in Grades 9–12. ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1969030))

**Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.** The KP government announced AI in the school curriculum from Grade 6 to Grade 12, with 35–50% AI-focused material across grade levels, and approved the recruitment of 7,555 IT teachers. The same report noted a shortage of trained teachers and IT labs. ([The Nation](https://www.nation.com.pk/31-Jan-2026/kp-introduce-ai-school-curriculum-grade-6))

**Punjab.** In September 2026, the Punjab Chief Minister directed officials to prepare an AI subject for Class 1 to Class 12, along with AI labs and weekly teacher training. ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/09/25/punjab-to-introduce-new-subject-from-class-1-to-12/))

Timelines and details differ by province and are still being worked out, so check the latest notification from your own education department or board.

## 2. What does this mean for your school?

Three things stand out from these announcements:

- **AI is becoming a school subject, not an extra.** Parents will soon expect every good school to teach it.
- **It is about building, not just using.** Robotics design, automation and problem solving mean students make things: programs, robots, smart devices.
- **Teachers are the bottleneck.** Every report points to a shortage of trained teachers and labs. Schools that train their teachers early will be ready first.

## 3. A five-step plan to get ready

### Step 1: Check what you teach today

List what each grade learns in computer class now. Many schools still teach typing and MS Office only. Compare that list with the six focus areas above and mark the gaps.

### Step 2: Put coding and computational thinking in every grade

Computational thinking is the base for AI and robotics. Students should move from logic games and block coding in the early grades to real programming by Grade 8. Our [ITEC curriculum](TK:itec) does this for Grades 1–8, with [Bit-by-Bit books](TK:books) and an [LMS](TK:lms).

### Step 3: Add hands-on robotics and IoT

Robotics design and automation are learned by building. Start with simple robot kits in the primary grades, then Arduino and sensors in Grades 6–8. Our [Robotics & IoT program](TK:robotics) uses VEX in Grades 1–5 and Arduino in Grades 6–8, with real student projects such as a smart dustbin and a line-following robot.

### Step 4: Train your teachers first

Your teachers do not need to be engineers or programmers, but they do need structured, hands-on training before they teach. Train a small group of lead teachers who can then support others. Our [AITEP program](TK:aitep) trains teachers to use AI for lesson planning and tests, and includes a Generative AI curriculum for students.

### Step 5: Measure progress

Check every term whether students can actually build and explain their projects. Simple assessments, classroom visits and grade-wise reports show what is working and where teachers need support. See our [monitoring and evaluation service](TK:mne).

## 4. Questions principals ask

**Do we need a new computer lab?** Not on day one. A working computer lab with internet is enough for coding and AI. Robotics kits can be added grade by grade.

**Can we start mid-year?** Yes. Start with one or two grades, or with teacher training, and expand the next session.

**What about Grades 9–12?** Coding and computational thinking from the earlier grades give students the base they need for AI and computer science electives later.
